# Code of Conduct

## Our Pledge

We — the contributors and maintainers of manim_extensions — are committed
to keeping this project a welcoming, respectful and productive place for
everyone, regardless of experience level, background or identity. We want
the project to be judged on its code and its ideas, not on who wrote them.

## Expected Behaviour

In all project spaces — issues, pull requests, discussions, documentation,
and any other place where this project's work happens — we ask everyone to:

- Be respectful of other people's time, effort and opinions.
- Give constructive feedback: say what can be improved and why, not just
  what is wrong.
- Assume good faith and ask for clarification before assuming bad intent.
- Credit others for their work and respect their authorship.
- Keep discussions on topic and focused on the code.

## Unacceptable Behaviour

The following are not tolerated:

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks of any kind.
- Discriminatory or derogatory language, including jokes based on identity.
- Publishing private information (such as email addresses or private
  communications) without explicit permission.
- Trolling, baiting, or deliberately derailing a discussion.
- Any other conduct that would reasonably be considered unprofessional.

## Enforcement

Maintainers are responsible for clarifying the standards above and for
taking appropriate and fair action when they are not met. This may include
removing or editing comments and other contributions, or temporarily or
permanently banning anyone who behaves in a way that is not acceptable.

Reports are handled confidentially. The identity of the reporter is kept
private wherever possible.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all project spaces, and also applies
when a member is representing the project in a public context — for
example, when speaking about the project at an event or on behalf of the
project in another community.

## Reporting

If you experience or witness unacceptable behaviour, please report it to
the maintainers. Reports can be sent privately to the project maintainers
on GitHub, or through any other contact channel the maintainers have made
public. When reporting, include as much detail as you can: what happened,
where, when, and who was involved. All reports are taken seriously.
